The Women Behind the Soap

From the tea slopes of Ambootia, Kurseong — a story of craft, community and quiet determination.

Who We Are

Grihine — "Of the Home"

Grihine comes from grihini — the homemaker. For generations, the women of the Darjeeling Hills have run their homes with skill the world rarely saw: which herb soothes a rash, which flower scents the water, how honey heals.

The Shree Ganesh Self Help Group decided that knowledge deserved more than the kitchen shelf. Pooling savings, recipes and courage, they began crafting small batches of soap from what grows around them — neem and mugwort from the forest edge, roses from home gardens, honey from hill apiaries, oranges from the valley orchards.

Today, every Grihine bar carries that inheritance. Made slowly. Made honestly. Made by women whose names you'd know if you visited — and who would insist you stay for tea.

Our Journey

From Kitchen Table to Your Doorstep

The Beginning

A committee is formed

Women of the Ambootia Tea Estate community come together, pooling traditional herbal knowledge and a shared dream of independent income.

First Batches

Soap-making begins at home

The first neem and honey bars are made over kitchen stoves and shared with neighbours. Word spreads from house to house, then village to village.

Growing Craft

20+ varieties and signature designs

The committee masters new recipes and hand-carved moulds — including the now-famous grape cluster soap strung on jute rope.

Today

Grihine reaches the world

Homestays, boutiques and families across India order Grihine. Every order flows straight back to the women of Kurseong — and the hills they call home.
